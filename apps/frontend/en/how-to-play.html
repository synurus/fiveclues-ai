How to Play

Five Clues is a word-guessing game. An AI clue-giver describes a secret word in five short clues without ever saying it, and you work out what it is. No sign-up, nothing to install — a game usually takes one to three minutes.

How a game goes

  1. Start — On the first screen, pick English, then press Play today's puzzle (see below) or Free play. Free play picks a random secret word and shows five clues that never say it. A nickname is optional; it's only sent if you leave feedback.
  2. Round 1 — The five clues appear one at a time. When you've read them, type the word you think it is and press Guess. You get one try.
  3. Round 2 — Missed it? The word's category is revealed (for example, Animals or Kitchenware) along with five more specific clues. Your round-1 clues and guess stay on screen, so you can look at everything together before your second guess.
  4. Result — Get it in round 1 and you'll see Nailed it!; in round 2, Nice!; miss both and it's So close! with the answer revealed.
  5. Feedback (optional) — On the result screen, tap 👍 on the clues that cracked it and 👎 on the useless ones. You can pick several and add a short comment.

If you keep playing in the same browser tab, the last 30 words won't come up again. Reloading the page resets that list.

Today's puzzle

One puzzle a day, and everyone gets the same word and the same clues. It follows your device's date, so a new puzzle arrives at your midnight. When you're done, Share result sends a spoiler-free summary to your friends.

  • 🟩 — got it in round 1
  • 🟥🟩 — missed round 1, got it in round 2
  • 🟥🟥 — missed both

Solve it two or more days in a row and your streak is shown too. Whether you've played today and your streak are saved only in this browser, so they don't carry over to other devices. Free play is always open, even after today's puzzle.

Why are the clues vague on purpose?

The AI follows one rule: every clue must be true of the word, but it must also fit two or three other things in the same category. If one clue gave the answer away, the game would be over instantly; if a clue fit everything in the category, it would tell you nothing.

So for “ramen” it won't mention “noodles” or “broth”, and for “airport” it won't mention “baggage claim” — those point straight at the answer. Instead, each clue leaks a trait shared by a few candidates, so the answer only becomes clear when you overlap all five.

An example game

This is an illustration of how a game flows. Real clues are generated fresh every time, so even the same word gets different clues.

Round 1 clues

  1. Home is somewhere cold
  2. Surprisingly quick in the water
  3. Lives in big groups
  4. Loves eating fish
  5. Always looks cute in photos

→ First guess: “seal” (wrong)

Round 2 — category Birds revealed

  1. Sways side to side when it walks
  2. Has wings, but the sky? Not really
  3. Keeps its egg on its feet
  4. Its belly is a different color from its back
  5. Sometimes slides across the ice on its belly

→ Second guess: “penguin” (correct)

Each round-1 clue also fits a seal, a walrus or a polar bear, so the first guess easily goes wrong. Once the category Birds appears and clue 7 (wings but can't fly) is added, the candidates collapse. A clue like 7 that narrows things down is a 👍 crucial clue; a clue like 5 that could describe almost anything is a 👎 useless clue.

What counts as correct

  • Case, spaces and punctuation are ignored. “Ice Cream” and “icecream” both match “ice cream”.
  • Plurals and articles are fine. “dolphins” and “a dolphin” both count for “dolphin”.
  • Common alternative names count. For example: refrigerator ↔ fridge, sofa ↔ couch, french fries ↔ chips, soccer ↔ football, smartphone ↔ cell phone.
  • A shorter form made of the answer's own words is accepted. For example, “fries” for “french fries”.
  • Naming a different word from the game is wrong, even if the letters overlap: “ant” is not “elephant”, and “bee” is not “beetle”.

What words come up?

The English version has about 470 everyday words across 24 categories. Near-identical pairs were trimmed to one (gloves stayed and mittens went; glasses stayed and sunglasses went), so a correct answer never comes down to luck.

Tips

  • Don't lock onto one clue. Each clue is written to fit several candidates. Look for the one answer that fits all five.
  • Start with the oddest clue. The clue that rules out the most candidates is usually the one that feels strangest.
  • In round 2, use the category. Check whether your round-1 idea even belongs to it.
  • Round 2 knows your wrong guess. The AI avoids clues that would lead back to it and narrows things down a step, so if round 2 feels different, it's steering you away from your first answer.
  • Type a short noun. “airplane” works; “the thing that flies” doesn't.

Where does feedback go?

Your 👍, 👎 and comments are collected once a day to improve the AI's instructions — for example, if a certain kind of generic clue keeps getting marked useless, the instructions are adjusted to avoid it. A human reviews every change before it goes live. More in About.

Feedback is stored as public issues in a GitHub repository that anyone can read. Please don't put your real name or contact details in the nickname or comment fields. See the Privacy Policy for details.
